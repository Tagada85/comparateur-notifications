Hosting, EU region and subprocessors

Where does the data you entrust to a notification tool live, and who else processes it? This page compares what five vendors publish on four points, based on their official pages collected on November 5, 2026. Vendors are presented in alphabetical order; the list is not exhaustive, and none pays to be on it.

Depending on your need

This site does not name a winner: the right tool depends on what you are looking for. The rows to read first:

Keep data in the European Union
“Data regions”, “Declared cloud provider”, “Subprocessor list”, “Company behind the service”: the region and the plan that opens it, then where the subprocessors are and which country’s jurisdiction the vendor falls under.

Why it is a compliance question

A notification tool receives personal data about your users: email address, identifier, message content. Under the GDPR, the vendor generally acts as your processor, and its own providers are subprocessors: Article 28 requires it to obtain your authorization before engaging them, and to inform you of any addition or replacement. The country of the hosting and that of the company behind the service matter for data transfers outside the European Union.

European Union region

Can the data be kept in the European Union, and on what condition?

Courier
Two separate datacenters, in the United States and in the EU (Ireland), with no shared data; the EU datacenter is available on paid plans (Business and Enterprise). (source, November 5, 2026)
Knock
Knock says it expects to offer data residency in the European Union by the end of 2026. (source, November 5, 2026)
MagicBell
The Security page claims GDPR compliance, without stating a hosting region. (source, November 5, 2026)
Novu
EU region in Frankfurt (Germany), chosen when the account is created; other regions (United Kingdom, Singapore, Australia, Japan, South Korea) on Enterprise plans. (source, November 5, 2026) Data residency in the United States or the EU is included from the free plan up. (source, November 5, 2026)
SuprSend
The Security page mentions data residency options in the EU, without naming a region. (source, November 5, 2026)

Declared cloud provider

The provider that hosts the service, when the vendor names it.

Courier
Amazon Web Services for cloud infrastructure, in the United States and in the EU (subprocessor list updated on August 3, 2026). (source, November 5, 2026)
Knock
Amazon Web Services for hosting and cloud infrastructure, in the United States (subprocessor list updated on June 10, 2026). (source, November 5, 2026)
MagicBell
The Security page mentions “secure cloud platforms”, without naming a hosting provider. (source, November 5, 2026)
Novu
The Security page of the documentation gives the regions, not the cloud provider. (source, November 5, 2026)
SuprSend
The Security page does not name a cloud provider. (source, November 5, 2026)

Subprocessor list

The public list of providers that process customer data, and where they are located.

Courier
Public list updated on August 3, 2026: 15 subprocessors, 9 of them located in “USA & EU” and 6 in the United States only. (source, November 5, 2026)
Knock
Public list updated on June 10, 2026: 19 subprocessors, all located in the United States. (source, November 5, 2026)
MagicBell
The Security page does not publish a subprocessor list; it mentions data processing agreements (DPAs). (source, November 5, 2026)
Novu
Novu’s trust center only renders with JavaScript: no subprocessor list can be read in the served page. (source, November 5, 2026)
SuprSend
The Security page has a “Data Subprocessors” section, but no list in the served text. (source, November 5, 2026)

Company behind the service

The company you contract with, and its country.

Courier
trycourier.com, Inc., San Francisco (United States). (source, November 5, 2026)
Knock
Knock Labs, Inc., New York (United States). (source, November 5, 2026)
MagicBell
MagicBell, Inc., San Francisco (United States). (source, November 5, 2026)
Novu
Noti-Fire Apps Ltd., Ramat Gan (Israel), with a representative in the EU under Article 27 of the GDPR. (source, November 5, 2026)
SuprSend
SuprStack Inc.; its terms of service are governed by the laws of Delaware (United States). (source, November 5, 2026)

How to read this information

  • A European region does not settle everything: also look at where the subprocessors are located, not just the main hosting provider.
  • When a page does not name the hosting provider or publish a list, the information may exist elsewhere: ask the vendor for its data processing agreement (DPA) and its subprocessor list.

What this comparison does not cover

This page is not legal advice. It does not compare certifications, the content of data processing agreements or transfer mechanisms. It relies on the vendors’ public pages; a trust center that only displays with JavaScript, or that refuses automated requests, could not be read. Has a piece of information changed? Report it.
