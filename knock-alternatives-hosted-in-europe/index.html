Knock alternatives hosted in Europe

Where Knock stands: Knock says it expects to offer data residency in the European Union by the end of 2026. (source, November 5, 2026) Amazon Web Services for hosting and cloud infrastructure, in the United States (subprocessor list updated on June 10, 2026). (source, November 5, 2026)

If your data must stay in the European Union before that date, this page shows who already offers it, then how much of Knock you will find at each of them, based on their pages collected from November 4 to 11, 2026. Vendors are presented in alphabetical order; the list is not exhaustive, and none pays to be on it.

Who offers to keep data in the European Union

Courier
Two separate datacenters, in the United States and in the EU (Ireland), with no shared data; the EU datacenter is available on paid plans (Business and Enterprise). (source, November 5, 2026)
Novu
EU region in Frankfurt (Germany), chosen when the account is created; other regions (United Kingdom, Singapore, Australia, Japan, South Korea) on Enterprise plans. (source, November 5, 2026) Data residency in the United States or the EU is included from the free plan up. (source, November 5, 2026)
SuprSend
The Security page mentions data residency options in the EU, without naming a region. (source, November 5, 2026)

MagicBell is not on this list: The Security page claims GDPR compliance, without stating a hosting region. (source, November 5, 2026) The region says neither where the subprocessors are located nor which country’s jurisdiction the vendor falls under: see hosting, EU region and subprocessors.

Workflows as code or via API

Your workflows may live in your repository and your CI.

At Knock: The Knock CLI lets you work on workflows and templates locally, then commit and promote changes from a CI/CD pipeline. (source, November 4, 2026)

Courier
Journeys (message sequences triggered by an event) are created through the API: a trigger, steps, an exit, then publishing. (source, November 5, 2026)
Novu
Novu Framework lets you write workflows in code and expose no-code controls to non-technical people. (source, November 4, 2026)
SuprSend
A management API to handle workflows, templates, schemas and translations programmatically, and to push or pull them from one workspace to another. (source, November 5, 2026)

What is billed

The same volume is not counted the same way from one vendor to another.

At Knock: The message sent: one message delivered to one user on one channel. A trigger that produces an in-app message and an email counts as two. (source, November 5, 2026)

Courier
The send: one template sent to one recipient. A multichannel template counts as a single send, even when it goes out by email, SMS and push; a send to a list of 100 users counts as 100. (source, November 5, 2026)
Novu
The workflow run: one execution of a workflow. Triggering a workflow to a topic of 100 subscribers counts as 100 runs. (source, November 5, 2026)
SuprSend
The notification: one message sent to a user or an object on one channel. A digest that groups several requests counts as one. (source, November 5, 2026)

Channels

The messaging apps your users already receive messages in.

At Knock: Email (built-in channel or providers such as Amazon SES, Postmark, SendGrid or SMTP), SMS, mobile push through APNs (iOS), Firebase Cloud Messaging (Android), Expo (React Native) and OneSignal, in-app feeds and in-app guides, and chat apps: Slack, Discord, Microsoft Teams and WhatsApp. (source, November 11, 2026)

Courier
Email, SMS, mobile push (iOS, Android, React Native) and web push, an in-app inbox (Courier Inbox), and chat apps: Slack, Microsoft Teams, WhatsApp, Discord, Facebook Messenger and Viber. (source, November 11, 2026)
Novu
In-app Inbox, email, SMS, push to mobile and web apps (mobile push and web push: FCM, APNs, Expo, OneSignal, Pushpad…) and chat apps: Slack, Discord, Microsoft Teams, WhatsApp Business, Telegram, Mattermost, Webex, Zulip… (source, November 11, 2026)
SuprSend
Email, SMS, WhatsApp, mobile push and web push, an in-app Inbox, Slack and Microsoft Teams, from a single API; Slack and Teams messages can go to your customers’ own workspaces. (source, November 11, 2026)

Courier
Client SDKs render the in-app interface in React, Vue, iOS, Android and Flutter. (source, November 4, 2026)

Notification center: preferences

The settings your users have already made.

At Knock: A guide to building a complete preference center with React and Knock’s preferences API. (source, November 4, 2026)

Courier
An embeddable preferences component: the signed-in user chooses which topics they receive and the channel for each. (source, November 4, 2026)
Novu
A Preferences view built into the Inbox: the subscriber sets their channels for all workflows or workflow by workflow. (source, November 4, 2026)
SuprSend
A ready-to-embed preferences page, synced with the categories defined in the dashboard, and hooks for a custom interface. (source, November 4, 2026)

Before migrating

What to take stock of on your side, whichever vendor you choose:

  • the workflows and message templates, and where they are versioned;
  • the preferences your users have saved, so as not to send them again what they have declined;
  • the notification center history, which your users expect to find again;
  • your monthly volume, recounted in the new vendor’s unit: how much does notification infrastructure cost?
